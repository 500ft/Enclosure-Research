# WMO (2023): Guide to Instruments and Methods of Observation (WMO-No. 8), Volume I

**Source:** https://library.wmo.int/records/item/68695-guide-to-instruments-and-methods-of-observation
**Evidence boundary:** International measurement guide/manual, not an experimental paper. Authoritative practice for meteorological instrument exposure, shielding, siting, and measurement uncertainty; it does not evaluate low-cost sensors specifically.

## Sensors

No specific models; covers measurement practice for temperature, humidity, pressure, wind, radiation, and precipitation instruments.

### Pros

- Defines standard exposure practice: screen/shield use, mounting height, and distance from surfaces and obstructions — directly applicable to this study's deployment setup section.
- States operational measurement-uncertainty requirements meteorological references are expected to meet, supporting the reference-uncertainty budget in the methods.
- Widely accepted baseline a reviewer will expect a sensor-siting methodology to acknowledge.

### Cons

- Written for operational/reference networks; some requirements are impractical for low-cost boxes and must be adapted, not adopted verbatim.
- Does not address low-cost sensor calibration transferability or enclosure material selection.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Covers radiation screens/shields and exposure geometry as practice guidance; it does not compare specific enclosure materials.

### Pros

- Gives the reference standard for what a properly exposed temperature/RH measurement looks like, anchoring the study's shield-vs-box comparisons.

### Cons

- No quantitative material data (absorptance, emissivity) for printed polymers; those remain to be measured in this study.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Supports the deployment-setup siting rules and the requirement to document reference conformance to WMO practice.
- Cannot justify specific low-cost enclosure geometries; it defines the target, not the low-cost path to it.

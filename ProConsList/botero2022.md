# Botero-Valencia et al. (2022): 3D-Printed Radiation Shields

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ohx.2022.e00267  
**Evidence boundary:** Direct comparison of exposed, PLA-shielded, and ASA-shielded SHT10 sensors, with outdoor weathering tests.

## Sensors

Three identical SHT10 temperature/RH sensors with outdoor covers; Vantage Pro reference; Particle Argon logger.

### Pros

- Identical sensors isolate the effect of enclosure selection.
- Combined temperature/RH sensing simplifies comparison.
- Outdoor covers improve sensor protection.

### Cons

- Results are tied to an older SHT10 sensor and may not transfer exactly to newer sensors.
- Sensor-to-sensor variation can still confound material comparison.
- Passive response remains dependent on ambient airflow.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Orange PLA and white ASA six-cone shields; five perforated cones, solid roof cone, 20 mm metal spacers, PVC mount, IP69 electronics box.

### Pros

- Cone-stack geometry blocks direct radiation while allowing passive ventilation.
- Modular cone count and spacing support controlled geometry experiments.
- ASA provides better UV/weather resistance than PLA.
- Separate IP69 box protects electronics.

### Cons

- PLA has weaker long-term outdoor durability.
- Color differs between PLA and ASA, so material and solar absorptance are not fully isolated.
- Many cones, spacers, and fasteners increase assembly effort.
- Passive ventilation may be insufficient during low-wind, high-solar conditions.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- This is the strongest source for selecting a white ASA passive shield as a comparison design.
- Future tests should control color when comparing materials and independently vary cone spacing or count.

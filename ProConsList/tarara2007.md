# Tarara and Hoheisel (2007): Low-Cost Shielding

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.21273/HORTSCI.42.6.1372  
**Evidence boundary:** Controlled field comparison of passive shield geometries made from the same reflective insulation.

## Sensors

The same temperature-sensor type was used in each low-cost shield and in the standard Gill comparison shield.

### Pros

- Holding sensor type constant isolates geometry effects.
- Temperature sensing is low power and suitable for distributed field experiments.

### Cons

- Conclusions concern radiation error, not sensor drift or long-term sensor durability.
- Results may change with sensor size, thermal mass, and surface emissivity.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Reflective foil insulation with high albedo and low emissivity; plate, stacked-plate, cone, double-cone, hanging-tube, rocket, pagoda, and perforated-tube geometries.

### Pros

- Same base material allows meaningful geometry comparison.
- Stacked plates produced the smallest daytime error.
- Low material cost and simple construction support dense deployments.

### Cons

- Open bottoms admitted reflected radiation and produced large warm errors.
- Tubes needed more than 30% sidewall perforation for adequate convection.
- All passive designs performed worst during low wind and high irradiance.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Prioritize overlapping stacked plates and quantify open-area ratio.
- Avoid open-bottom and poorly perforated tube geometries.
- Test reflected radiation from the ground, not only direct sunlight.

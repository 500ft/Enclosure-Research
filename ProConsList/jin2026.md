# Jin et al. (2026): High-Accuracy Naturally Ventilated Shield

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos17030272  
**Evidence boundary:** CFD material/geometry optimization and outdoor validation of a recent near-surface temperature shield.

## Sensors

Thin-film Pt100 housed in an 8-mm copper spherical shell.

### Pros

- Pt100 supports accurate and stable temperature measurement.
- Spherical shell enables modeled heat-transfer behavior.

### Cons

- Copper shell can absorb radiation and adds thermal mass.
- Precision readout is more complex than common digital sensors.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Two 200-mm square shading plates; symmetric bowl-cover flow-guiding shrouds with 30-mm spacing; CFD comparisons of plastic, wood, Fe-Ni alloy, and aluminum; mirror-finish aluminum sunshade combinations.

### Pros

- Geometry addresses downward solar and upward ground radiation.
- Curved shrouds guide airflow through the internal cavity.
- Material comparisons explicitly connect thermal properties to error.
- Natural ventilation avoids fan power.

### Cons

- Large and complex geometry may be harder to manufacture and mount.
- CFD assumptions require validation under broader weather conditions.
- Mixed materials and mirror finishes introduce weathering and maintenance questions.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Include upward/reflected radiation and airflow guidance in geometry criteria.
- Consider manufacturability and coating durability alongside modeled accuracy.

# Deford et al. (2025): 3D-Printed Aspirated Radiation Shield

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1175/JTECH-D-24-0006.1  
**Evidence boundary:** Open-source aspirated-housing design and outdoor comparison with passive and commercial aspirated shields.

## Sensors

Compact air-temperature sensor housed within an aspirated unit; Sunon MF40100V1-1000U-A99 fan.

### Pros

- Forced airflow reduces dependence on ambient wind.
- Design accepts multiple compact sensor types.
- Performance was comparable to more expensive shield options.

### Cons

- Fan consumes 680 mW and creates an autonomy penalty.
- Fan and exposed sensor circuits add failure points.
- Sensor dimensions are limited to approximately 2.7 x 1 cm.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

White PLA; three printed parts; 90-degree elbow inlet, concentric double-wall air gap, insect screen, central supports, and angled fan diffuser; hot glue during testing.

### Pros

- Elbow blocks direct radiation and precipitation.
- Double wall reduces conductive heating.
- Printed geometry is customizable and easy to reproduce.
- Insect screen and centered sensor improve field usability.

### Cons

- PLA, hot glue, fan, and exposed circuits limit long-term durability.
- Printing took approximately 19 hours and 130 g of filament.
- Orientation effects and extreme wet/salty environments were not fully tested.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Use this as the active-aspiration comparison design.
- Measure fan power and failure behavior; consider ASA/PETG, permanent joints, and conformal coating.

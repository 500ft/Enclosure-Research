# García Izquierdo et al. (2024): Arctic Shield Intercomparison

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos15070841  
**Evidence boundary:** Fourteen-month Arctic comparison of ten shield models and 41 calibrated thermometers.

## Sensors

Calibrated four-wire Pt100 thermometers, including black high-emissivity probes; complete manufacturer thermometer/logger systems; ultrasonic anemometers and reference meteorology.

### Pros

- Identical calibrated Pt100 probes isolate shield effects.
- Redundant and black probes reveal internal thermal homogeneity and residual radiation.
- Pre/post calibration supports drift assessment.

### Cons

- Shield performance remains dependent on the thermometer-shield combination.
- Active shields require validated aspiration rate and power monitoring.
- High-quality Pt100 systems are more expensive than typical low-cost sensors.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Ten natural and active shield models, including multiplate and Stevenson-screen arrangements; detailed proprietary materials vary.

### Pros

- Long deployment captures seasonal and extreme-weather behavior.
- Directly compares passive and active ventilation.
- Large shields with good natural ventilation performed strongly in Arctic conditions.

### Cons

- One shield disappeared in a storm; another thermometer fell from its mount.
- Fan, power, logger, memory, and remote-configuration failures caused data loss.
- Commercial materials and internal geometry are not fully disclosed.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Use redundant probes and log fan operation, power, and storage status.
- Include mount retention, storm resistance, and memory capacity in autonomy testing.

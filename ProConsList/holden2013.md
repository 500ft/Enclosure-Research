# Holden et al. (2013): Inexpensive Folded Radiation Shield

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2013.06.011  
**Evidence boundary:** Three-site outdoor comparison against commercial passive shields, RAWS, and an aspirated ASOS reference.

## Sensors

Low-cost temperature sensors, including LogTag units in the reported comparisons.

### Pros

- Low-cost sensors enable fine-scale distributed temperature mapping.
- Performance was adequate for many ecological and forestry applications when shielded.

### Cons

- Sensor precision limits the achievable comparison accuracy.
- Full-sun, low-wind conditions still caused warm bias.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Approximately USD 3 Gill-style shield made by folding white corrugated-plastic sheets into a series of plates.

### Pros

- Very low cost, fast construction, and easy modification.
- Performed similarly to a more expensive commercial passive shield.
- White multiplate geometry blocks radiation while permitting airflow.

### Cons

- Corrugated plastic durability and UV life were not the main focus.
- Orientation and folding consistency can affect repeatability.
- Passive ventilation cannot remove low-wind radiation error.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Include a folded white-sheet shield as a low-cost manufacturability benchmark.
- Compare build repeatability and weathering, not only initial accuracy.

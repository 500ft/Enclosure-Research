# feinberg2018: Long-term Evaluation of Air Sensor Technology under Ambient Conditions in Denver, Colorado

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-11-4605-2018
**Evidence boundary:** Experimental field-evaluation paper (Atmos. Meas. Tech. 11(8):4605–4615, 2018; EPA CAIRSENSE-Denver). Seven-month collocation of low-cost PM, O3, and NO2 sensors deployed in triplicate against FEM monitors at an urban regulatory site (2015–2016, high-altitude cool dry climate). Abstract-level facts verified from journal metadata (2026-07); per-sensor completeness and correlation numbers must be pulled from the full text before being quoted.

## Sensors

Commercial low-cost PM, ozone, and NO2 sensor packages (models listed in the paper; not re-verified here), deployed in triplicate.

### Pros

- Evaluates **data completeness explicitly** as a long-term performance metric alongside correlation and trend reproduction — direct precedent for this manuscript's Section 5.3 completeness reporting.
- Triplicate deployment separates unit-to-unit variability from sensor-class behavior — relevant to this project's two-unit question (Log A vs Log B).
- FEM collocation at a regulatory site is the same reference architecture this manuscript's Section 4 specifies.

### Cons

- Denver's high-altitude dry climate limits transfer of specific performance numbers to a New York deployment.
- Enclosure/packaging effects are not isolated from sensor behavior.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Sensor packages as shipped by their manufacturers; enclosure detail not re-verified here and not a study variable.

### Pros

- Realistic outdoor exposure over seven months, including seasonal transitions.

### Cons

- No enclosure-design evidence; do not cite for material or geometry selection.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Supports reporting completeness/uptime as a metric of record for low-cost AQ deployments (Section 5.3) and evaluating sensors across seasons rather than in a single window.
- Supports deploying more than one identical unit to bound unit-to-unit variability — strengthening the case for clarifying whether Logs A and B are two units.
- Cannot justify enclosure material or geometry decisions.
